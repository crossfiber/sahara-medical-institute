The parent in the room should never feel translated.

Dr. Francisco Peraza, founder

Dr. Peraza opened Sahara Medical Institute in 2007 after years as a First-Degree Pediatric Specialist in Havana and a Nevada license earned the same year. He built the practice around one idea: every conversation about a fever, a feeding question, a vaccine schedule, or a behavioral concern happens in the language that family thinks in.

Nineteen years on, the practice is four providers strong, accepting newborns and new patients, and still on West Sahara Avenue. Walk-ins for sick visits, telemedicine when you can't leave the house, and a team that learned this from Dr. Peraza first: take the extra ten minutes, explain it again, write it down if it helps.

Your first visit

What it looks like, start to finish.

Before you come in

Bring three things.

Your insurance card, a photo ID, and your child's immunization record if you have one. For a transfer from another pediatrician, ask them to fax records to (702) 818-5013 ahead of time. Masks are required to enter the facility.

When you arrive

Front desk speaks Spanish.

Tell them whether you're here for a scheduled visit or a walk-in. For sick walk-ins, you'll see the first available provider on a first-come, first-served basis. You're welcome to request a specific provider if you'd rather schedule.

On your way out

You leave with a plan.

Whatever the visit is, you leave with the next step written down. Refill instructions in plain language, vaccine schedule on paper, a follow-up date if needed. We answer phone calls from established families for clarification afterward.
